    Money words, explained

    Gross pay
    Your pay before anything is taken out. A "$50,000 salary" is gross pay.
    Take-home (net) pay
    What actually lands in your bank account after taxes and deductions. This budget is built on take-home pay.
    FICA
    Social Security (6.2%) and Medicare (1.45%) taxes. Every paycheck pays these; there's no standard deduction for them.
    EIT & LST
    Pennsylvania local taxes. The Earned Income Tax is a % of your pay set by where you live (about 1% around East Stroudsburg). The Local Services Tax is a small flat fee (up to $52/yr) set by where you work.
    401(k) & employer match
    A retirement account through your job. A "match" means your employer adds money when you contribute: free money you should always collect.
    Roth IRA
    A retirement account you open yourself. You put in money you've already paid tax on; it grows and comes out tax-free after age 59½. Contributions (not earnings) can be withdrawn anytime.
    High-yield savings account (HYSA)
    A savings account, usually at an online bank, that pays much more interest than a regular one. The right home for an emergency fund.
    Index fund
    A low-cost fund that owns hundreds or thousands of companies at once, so you don't have to pick stocks. A simple, proven way to invest for the long run.
    Sinking fund
    Money you set aside a little at a time for a known future expense, like a car down payment or apartment deposit.
    Credit utilization
    How much of your credit card limit you're using. Keeping it under ~30% (ideally under 10%) and paying in full every month builds a strong credit score.
